# Translate Tujia / 土家语翻译

Build a complete, usable translation from attested words and constructions. Preserve the source's meaning and distinguish supported forms, productive combinations, and unresolved choices. Evidence gaps affect confidence labels, **not whether the requested translation is delivered**.

## Mandatory completion and retrieval boundaries (highest priority within this skill)

1. **Always deliver a translation.** Whenever this skill is invoked to translate, the final answer MUST include a proposed Tujia rendering of **every source sentence, line, and meaningful clause**. Never end with only research results, a list of problems, a structural explanation, an apology, or “不确定／找不到，所以无法翻译”. Do not ask the user whether to continue. A translation with openly flagged uncertainty is better than withholding the translation. For audit-only or explanation-only requests, follow the requested task rather than inventing a translation.
2. **No web search actions.** The only lookups allowed during a translation are the designated Bizic/Tujia database and retrieval interface (`tujia-lex`, its local dictionary/corpus/grammar, the **`../tujia-synonym-lite/SKILL.md` no-model semantic-bridge subskill**, and **direct known-path GitHub connector file reads of generated Bizic static lexicon shards when needed**), plus materials supplied directly by the user. Reproducible installation/download of the known `tujia-lex` application, pinned BGE checkpoint and public dictionary inside a capable local/Codex runtime is allowed setup, **not permission to do exploratory web searches**. **Do not invoke web search, browser search, “Searched the web”, web.run, search engines, image search, online literature discovery, or exploratory browsing of external sites** to supplement a translation. Reading the specific manifest and static shard files is an authorized database lookup, not a web search; do not use general GitHub/web search or arbitrary browsing as a substitute for database queries. If an external source is missing, mark the gap rather than searching online. This restriction applies even when another source might be helpful.
3. **Choose and actually execute a retrieval mode.** If the environment supports downloading/installing and running the `tujia-lex` application with its BGE model and compatible persistent full index (e.g. a writable local/Codex workspace with Python, network access and sufficient resources), **use real embedding retrieval**; install/build **once and reuse assets** when necessary and feasible. If the environment is read-only, tool-only, offline, lacks model execution, or setup fails, **invoke `../tujia-synonym-lite/SKILL.md`** and perform bounded concept expansion followed by real exact queries against Bizic's versioned UTF-8 lexicon shards. Do not spend an ordinary translation turn repeatedly rebuilding or troubleshooting expensive assets; use the fallback immediately when model execution is not feasible. An old evaluation log or an installed package without a successful current query is **not** embedding retrieval. **Tell the user the actual mode at the beginning and in the final answer; no mislabeled scores.**
4. **Fallback until every span is rendered.** Prefer (a) verified native forms and productive syntax, then (b) meaning-preserving native paraphrase, then (c) clearly signaled Chinese lexical insertions/borrowings in attested Tujia phrase or clause order, and finally (d) an explicitly provisional, best-supported clause marked `⟦待核：…⟧` for unresolved morphology or structure. Keep the translated clause present; put the uncertainty marker or a short note beside it, not in place of the entire clause. Do not invent Tujia words, tones, or pronunciations to satisfy completion. **Completion means full semantic coverage as a proposed translation, not a claim of verification.**

## Staged user-visible progress updates (mandatory for nontrivial translations)

Do not work silently through a long retrieval and drafting process. **Send useful, user-visible updates as work progresses**, before the final translation. For multi-sentence, multi-paragraph, or otherwise nontrivial tasks, provide at least two substantive intermediate updates when there are distinct stages of work. Never leave the user without an update for roughly 20 seconds while work is ongoing. For a very short translation that can be completed immediately, deliver the result without artificial delays or filler.

- **Stage 1 — Scope, mode and evidence plan (early).** **Explicitly state `检索模式：Embedding（BGE 实际运行）` or `检索模式：Skill-based（概念扩展＋静态词典精确查询）` according to what can genuinely execute; if still checking availability, say `正在验证检索模式`, then announce the confirmed mode in the next update.** Briefly confirm the requested dialect, spelling, and main source priority (Brassett 2006 before 2004 for Longshan), and the first lexical or syntactic targets. Give an initial result if one is already established. Do not repeat the request as an empty status message.
- **Stage 2 — Verified retrieval results.** Share concrete findings as soon as available: a few high-value words or constructions with their actual source, a relevant clause pattern, or an honestly reported retrieval limitation and fallback. Do not claim evidence or embedding success that has not actually been obtained. **Include the first proposed translated sentence or passage as soon as it can be responsibly drafted**, even if other spans remain unchecked.
- **Stage 3 — Translation in progress.** For longer texts, expose additional translated passages incrementally, identify any localized uncertainty or Chinese insertions, and say which portion is still being checked. Deliver intermediate artifacts, not a mere percentage, timer, or list of searches. Do not reprint every earlier passage in updates unless necessary to correct it.
- **Final — Complete rendering and method disclosure.** Deliver every source sentence/line in order and include a single visible `检索模式` line stating **Embedding / Skill-based / Mixed** based on *actual* queries, with a brief reason for fallback and actual query IDs / source evidence when asked. Do not print cosine scores in Skill-based mode.  Earlier partial translations do not excuse missing later sentences. The final answer must stand on its own, with all necessary spans available and provisional wording clearly marked.

Updates can use short progress messages or partial answers, whichever the interface supports, but **must be visible to the user during the task**, not collected into one end-of-task report. Make each update accurate, brief, and additive; avoid fabricated progress, detailed private reasoning, and recurring "still searching" messages. The no-web-search restriction applies to every stage. If retrieval stalls, announce the switch to the available local fallback and continue translating immediately. Do not postpone delivery for optional research.

## Retrieval-mode decision and required disclosure

**Run this decision at the start of every translation; do not silently fall back.**

- **Embedding (preferred when executable).** In a writable local/Codex/runtime environment where Python tooling, downloads, RAM and persistent index storage are available, run the repository's **documented, pinned** setup if the application/index is not yet installed, then use `tujia-lex search ... --mode semantic --model-path ...` for high-value terms and constructions. Reuse the already built, schema-compatible model/index across queries. Inspect `manifest.json` for the exact source revision, full dictionary attestation coverage, model/checksum and dimensions; confirm the current query really encoded and returned genuine scores and records. If a required download is blocked or setup cannot reasonably complete, switch modes immediately. Do not use Python's remembered synonyms, a static JSON file or a historical BGE evaluation as proof that embedding ran.
- **Skill-based (mandatory no-model fallback).** Read `../tujia-synonym-lite/SKILL.md` and `../tujia-synonym-lite/concepts.json`. For unknown expressions, generate candidate expansions in context even when no curated entry exists. Lookup literal witness rows through `scripts/lightweight-synonym-search.py` if Python and static shards are mounted; otherwise compute the FNV-1a bucket and directly read the specific GitHub text shard(s) listed in `../translate-tujia/lexicon/manifest.json`. Expand an abstract compound to everyday actions, participant, head noun, property, relation, outcome and real paraphrases **before** considering Chinese insertion: e.g. `经济损失` → `钱` / `赔偿` / `经济` (related, not interchangeable), `玩具` → `玩` + `东西`, `饮料` → `喝` + action-to-object nominalization. Look up **all actually used candidates**, inspect source-linked witnesses, and test the grammatical combination. A no-hit literal compound is **not** a no-hit semantic field.
- **User-facing wording at the first substantive update and final** must be factual and unambiguous: `检索模式：Embedding（BGE；实际执行本次向量检索）` or `检索模式：Skill-based（近义概念扩展＋词典精确检索；没有运行 embedding）`. If both genuinely ran for separate spans, say `检索模式：Mixed` and identify which queries used which method. Never display invented cosine similarities, vector ranks, hit counts or unattested dialect attribution.
- **Query audit for test/debug requests:** show original and expanded queries, raw witness IDs, source, form and sense, and accepted/rejected alternatives. Embedding mode also reports real `score` from the current search. Skill-based mode reports no cosine score and labels relations (synonym/paraphrase/component/hypernym/example) clearly.
- **Bounded retrieval:** normally reuse the static shards and cached source records; a model/index installation is a one-time setup in capable environments, not something to repeat per sentence. No web search actions for translation, and no speculative claims that GitHub text shards are semantic vectors.

## Efficient grammar lookup and static dictionary fallback

- **Lookup-first grammar router:** Read **`references/grammar-index.md`** before researching unfamiliar grammar. It maps Chinese/English/Japanese *semantic triggers* to one short file rather than forcing a full grammar reread. Then consult **only the relevant subsection** of `references/aspect-modality.md` (快要、还、已经、能、想等), `references/event-linking.md` (liex/mor/bor, event sequencing, instruments, time/condition), or `references/clause-patterns.md` (negation, predicate types, property words, nominalization/classifiers). For a missing or conflicting pronunciation, consult `references/notation-audit.md`. All reference examples use **调号拼音 followed by source-aligned IPA**, never unrequested Ye. Preserve source provenance: Brassett 2006 first for Longshan, other Northern varieties and Southern explicitly separate. **Reuse attested examples and productive operations; search the designated corpus only for new lexical scopes, unattested combinations, source contradictions or dialect questions.** Do not label a teaching combination or supplementary example as Brassett-attested.
- **Do not attempt to read the binary `site/dictionary.json.gz` with a text-only GitHub connector.** Its failure to decompress is a known interface limitation, not evidence that dictionary data are unavailable. Use the checked-in UTF-8 static shards under `skills/translate-tujia/lexicon/` as the GitHub-text-only fallback. Read `lexicon/manifest.json`, normalize the Chinese/English/Tujia query using NFKC + casefold + trim, compute its portable FNV-1a UTF-8 hash and read only the `buckets[hex]` JSONL files. Each matching line has `k` equal to the normalized query and a compact `r` array; interpret its positions using the manifest's `record_fields` (entry ID, attestation ID, source, form, Chinese/English senses, POS, dialect, locator, source reference and **same-attestation** notations). Full original attestation objects remain in the canonical gzip/local semantic index. A text-only tool can calculate the bucket from the normalized UTF-8 query using FNV-1a, read only the listed text shard, then compare `k` exactly. Use `manifest.sources[r[2]].canonical_id` to distinguish Brassett (2006/2004) from other sources instead of ranking opaque source IDs by intuition. The source catalog is generated from the **same revision** of `site/data.json.gz` as the dictionary; if a source is not mapped, mark its scope unknown. **Never read every shard for one query.** When a local Python runtime already has the static files, use `python scripts/export-readable-lexicon.py lookup --directory skills/translate-tujia/lexicon --query '好'`. For full corpus, use the existing local data and tools.
- **Availability and provenance checks:** static shards are generated from a versioned public Bizic dictionary snapshot, not live by assumption. **Before raw fallback, run the dedicated `../tujia-synonym-lite/SKILL.md` query-expansion procedure; don't search only the unknown full compound.** Verify the manifest revision, SHA-256 and entry/attestation totals if source freshness or coverage matters; mention a stale or missing export rather than claiming the latest data. Prefer the actual `tujia-lex` full index when already installed. The fallback is **literal lexical evidence**, not a semantic embedding query.
- **Anti-abuse / DDoS design:** never deploy a public endpoint that decompresses or scans the gzip dictionary, performs embedding inference, builds an index, or queries all records per incoming request. Static artifacts are CDN-cacheable with bounded text shard sizes, no server execution or secret keys. Keep routine translation to a small handful of targeted shard reads, deduplicate requests and reuse cached results. Refuse unbounded enumeration or repeated large-scale fetching; suggest local/offline bulk processing instead. Static hosting reduces expensive request amplification but is not a guarantee against volumetric network attacks.
- **Source completeness:** an embedding run is not verified merely because the process exited successfully. The index manifest must report counts for all entries/attestations in the selected dictionary revision; attestations are not dropped when grouping compatible senses. Chinese and English sense glosses both receive semantic vectors; forms, original IPA, tones, notation links, source and locality remain in exact-match fields and inspectable records. When the model or vector index is unavailable, use the static/local literal fallback and continue to a complete proposed translation.

## Default translation notation (mandatory)

- **Every translated sentence or line defaults to TWO synchronized renderings: (1) 调号拼音, and (2) IPA.** Always output the site's tone-marked Tujia romanization **first**, then the corresponding IPA on the next line (or a two-column, clause-aligned table for longer passages). Do this for Northern/Longshan and Southern translation alike; keep the same word and clause boundaries so the reader can match the forms. `调号拼音` means the site's **tone-marked spelling convention**, not toneless romanization or the source's raw Ye spelling. IPA must include verified tones and relevant phonemic distinctions.
- **Never output 叶氏拼音 (Ye romanization) by default.** Show it **only when the user explicitly requests it**. When requested, label it clearly and keep the requested relationship to 调号拼音 / IPA; do not silently substitute Ye for either default line. A user's explicit alternative notation/output instruction can override this default for that request.
- **Convert from verified, source-linked pairs only.** Use the original record's matching notation fields and the site's source-specific `notation-profiles.js`, `grammar-notation.js`, `southern-notation.js` or `tujia-lex-spelling.cjs` when available. A Ye spelling found in Brassett is evidence to transform, **not** permission to print Ye as the translation. Never derive IPA from unverified one-to-one letter guesses, invent tones, or silently conflate different dialectal transcriptions.
- **Incomplete notation does not justify omitting a rendering.** Preserve the complete proposed translation in the available notation, supply verified IPA and tones for supported spans, and mark the exact unverified correspondence as `⟦待核调号⟧` or `⟦待核IPA⟧` without pretending it is known. Label Chinese lexical insertions in both aligned lines; do not invent Tujia IPA for Chinese insertions. Explain material notation gaps briefly **after** the translation. A missing conversion must not become an excuse to stop the translation or to fall back to Ye as the displayed result.
- **Progress updates** may initially share verified lexical pairs or partial translated passages, but should provide both synchronized lines for each passage once drafted. The final response always presents all translated passages in the requested/default notation with complete semantic coverage.

Example layout (schematic placeholders, not attested words):

```text
1. 调号拼音：〈译文中的调号拼音〉
   IPA：/〈同一句的 IPA〉/
```

**Navigation invariant:** consult the small router first, not all reference files. A common grammar meaning already covered by a source-backed example is not a reason to rerun embedding searches or read every grammar chapter. Keep the answer complete and give substantive progress updates.

## Establish the task

- Follow the requested dialect and locality. Reuse an unambiguous target from the conversation; if unspecified, default provisionally to Northern/Longshan and state that assumption once **without pausing for clarification**. For Northern without a narrower target, use Longshan materials and state that basis once. Translate Northern and Southern separately when both are requested.
- Identify the audience, register, and any **explicit** notation override; otherwise set the output to **调号拼音 + IPA**, never Ye romanization. Separate meaning from additional constraints such as meter, rhyme, line count, or terminology; explain a trade-off only when it affects the result.
- For revision, preserve the supplied text and edit the requested span. Distinguish the author's choices from independently attested forms.

## Find evidence

Start with the committed, locally available repository materials and the designated Tujia retrieval database. User-supplied materials may also be used with their provenance and access restrictions. Do not search the web for additional sources or fetch them from external sites during translation.

### Default source priority for Northern / Longshan (mandatory)

- **Check Brassett first for every Northern/Longshan translation**, including songs and short phrases. Consult Brassett et al. (2006) as the primary lexical and grammatical reference and Brassett (2004) as a supplementary witness **before** selecting forms from YuBao survey points, other Northern collections, or machine-generated glosses. This is the default, not an optional source preference. Explicit user source requirements and reliable evidence specific to the requested locality take precedence; do not silently mix different varieties.
- Search both Brassett sources for the actual intended sense, related senses, collocations, and example constructions, not merely a Chinese headword. Inspect attestation-linked spellings, original glosses, source citations, tone, and grammatical behavior. A high-ranked semantic match from another source must not displace an appropriate Brassett form without a concrete reason. If 2004 and 2006 differ, compare the original witnesses; favor the better-supported, more mature 2006 analysis rather than mechanically adopting an older transcription.
- Expand to explicitly localized Longshan evidence, continuous texts, and other Northern surveys **only after** Brassett has been checked or when they supply a better-documented local form or construction. Record which spans are Brassett-based and which come from another source; explain meaningful conflicts, gaps, and dialect differences. "Northern" without an attested locality is not automatically "Longshan".
- **Never manufacture a word from the apparent tail of a sentence or its gloss alignment.** An isolated symbol or segment such as /ɸ⁵⁵/ is not independently verified as a lexical syllable or a component of "love"; confirm the full source transcription, segment boundaries, and lexical meaning before reuse. If attestation, tone, or alignment remains doubtful, use a verified paraphrase or a clearly identified Chinese insertion; if even the structure is uncertain, provide a marked provisional rendering and localize the uncertainty in a note. Never leave the corresponding source clause without a proposed translation. Rhythmic constraints do not override this check.

In the standalone `bizic-translator` project, the paths below are relative to the public-data checkout supplied as `--repo` (normally `.cache/bizic-data`), rather than the application directory. Follow the README to fetch that fixed source snapshot.

For a story, craft, custom, or other identifiable topic, first locate related continuous texts by title, alternate names, and subject in both the structured corpus and source metadata. A source can be present even when it has not been segmented into corpus entries. Read relevant passages alongside the dictionary: continuous text can establish a contextual sense, a conventional name, a collocation, or a complete predicate that isolated headwords miss.

| Material | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `docs/reference-grammar.md` | Locate the relevant dialect's sentence patterns, nominal phrases, predicate forms, tone and spelling rules. Record the cited work, page, and example when available. |
| `site/dictionary.json.gz` | Search lexical senses and full forms. Inspect individual `attestations` and their IDs, source, locality, spelling scheme, and linked `notations`; merged entries can contain different witnesses. |
| `site/data.json.gz` | Look up source metadata and corpus texts. Check extraction quality, dialect, locality, and alignment before quoting an example. |
| `site/longshan-texts.json` | Find Northern discourse and genre-specific usage. Inspect `blocks`, literal wording, and context; a paragraph summary or cultural paraphrase is not a word-for-word translation. |
| `site/yubao-*.json` | Consult vocabulary and sentences from a specific survey point. Read each file's `point`, `location`, and `dialect`; Longshan, Xianren, Wanmi-po, and Southern Tanxi remain distinct. |
| `site/notation-profiles.js`, `site/grammar-notation.js`, `site/southern-notation.js` | Check source-specific transcription and display conversions. A display convention does not establish phonetic identity between sources. |

Decode gzip files before parsing, for example:

```python
import gzip, json
with gzip.open("site/dictionary.json.gz", "rt", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    words = json.load(f)["words"]
```

Search both lexical records and sentences; inspect complete records rather than taking the first gloss match. A missing search result establishes a retrieval gap. Machine glosses, OCR, editorial analyses, and a source cited by the grammar retain their limitations; cite a secondary quotation honestly when the original has not been checked. Record the repository commit for a reproducible audit.

When a likely source has broken extraction, inspect relevant original pages or images **if they are already available in the local dataset** before concluding that an expression is absent; do not fetch them from the web during translation.

Common construction decisions should be resolved from the prechecked subskill before launching fresh database retrieval; reserve the latter for missing or conflicting evidence. Interleaved transcription and glosses may extract in the wrong order. Record the printed page and PDF page separately. In parallel texts, distinguish the original sentence, its word glosses, and its free translation; literary expansions in the free translation need not correspond to individual original words. Extract a construction with its arguments, predicate, suffixes, and immediate context, rather than collecting disconnected equivalents.

Check source scope and version. A source identified only as Northern is not thereby verified as Longshan. A related legend can supply expressions for shared scenes while differing in names, relationships, causes, or ending. Preserve the requested version and identify which spans the comparison actually supports. Keep source transcriptions separate until locality and notation compatibility have been checked.

### Use the local retrieval tools

Use `tujia-lex` in an **installed or provisionable** executable environment; provision a pinned application/model/index **once** in a capable local/Codex workspace as described in `README.md`, then reuse it. In a tool-only/non-executable environment use `../tujia-synonym-lite/SKILL.md`, not a pretend BGE run. Inspect the index manifest before relying on semantic results. It must identify the actual pretrained model, revision, pooling, checksums, and source snapshot. If the index is unbuilt, model load fails, or a checkpoint is missing, do not claim a semantic run; use available literal queries and local dictionary/corpus files instead, then deliver the translation. Report the technical limitation briefly after the answer; do not turn a translation request into environment installation or troubleshooting.

Keep these retrieval tasks distinct:

| Task | Command pattern | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Literal lexical lookup | `tujia-lex search '守候' --index INDEX --mode exact` | Whole-field match; `--substring` is a separately labelled literal baseline. |
| Lexical semantic candidates | `tujia-lex search '守候' --index INDEX --model-path MODEL --source brassett-2004 --dialect north` | Real embedding neighbors of Chinese sense glosses; inspect each witness before choosing a word. |
| Construction candidates | `tujia-lex search '用刀切菜，工具在动作前面' --index INDEX --model-path MODEL --kind construction --dialect north` | Sentence blocks, extracted passages, and grammar sections, with distinct record types and evidence levels. |
| Original record | `tujia-lex inspect --index INDEX RECORD_ID` | Original forms, attestation links, transcription, source, scope, and citation. |

Follow the requested source preference explicitly. For Northern/Longshan translations by default, run source-filtered searches with `--source brassett-2006` and `--source brassett-2004` first, inspect original witnesses, and only then broaden to other Northern collections. Do not treat a cross-source embedding ranking as source priority. For a survey point, use `--point`; `--location` only matches an explicitly recorded locality. A dictionary's empty locality field remains unknown. `--pos` uses the source's own label; `--record-type` can separate an attested sentence from secondary grammar discussion or an extracted bilingual paragraph.

Use semantic retrieval to generate candidates, then check the intended sense and grammatical role against the original attestation. Inspect lower-ranked useful candidates and higher-ranked distractors. Common traps include a shared character with a different sense, a compound whose meaning is not compositional, a related object instead of an action, an antonym, and a suffix instead of a free verb. Broad queries can be decomposed into the actual action, participant, or relation needed in the sentence; record the new query and any change of meaning. Do not turn retrieved neighbors into an automatic synonym dictionary, infer equivalence from a cosine score, or choose a different botanical species merely because it ranks highest.

For construction retrieval, formulate the required relation as well as the scene: an action as the object of learning, a recipient of speech, instrument plus action, prior completion, maintained background state, or volition. Inspect the complete original and its citation. Shared subject matter alone does not license a construction. Cultural notes and free summaries are not token-aligned translations; extracted bilingual paragraphs require line-alignment checking. Grammar sections are secondary evidence, with sources to consult when needed. A sentence from another point remains evidence from that point.

Before delivering a longer translation, track internally each sentence's key lexical record IDs, applicable construction IDs, productive substitutions, and uncertain spans. Distinguish a lexical coverage gap, an unsuccessful retrieval, a missing local form or tone, an uncertain grammatical operation, and incomplete source data. Mark unresolved analyses without omitting the translated clause; a successful retrieval run does not certify the resulting translation.

## Validate grammatical applicability first

Before composing a clause or adopting a paraphrase, verify the grammatical operations it needs in the target variety. **Classify source-language adverbs and auxiliaries semantically before looking them up as separate vocabulary:** e.g. '快要做' (imminent onset) vs '快做完' (imminent endpoint); '还在' vs '还没' vs '还不必'; '会' (trained skill, possibility, prediction) vs '可以' (permission); '已经' (current result) vs '做过' (experience). Follow `references/grammar-index.md` to the relevant aspect/modality, event, or clause subsection first; use `references/notation-audit.md` if spellings disagree. Prioritize modality, aspect and stem changes, negation, nominalization, predicate class, and relations between linked events. For each operation, identify its intended meaning and scope, the class of predicate or phrase it selects, its attachment site and order, and any restrictions on combinations. Consult the relevant grammar discussion and its examples; a dictionary gloss or a list of suffixes establishes neither applicability nor co-occurrence.

| Area | Check before use |
| --- | --- |
| Modality | Distinguish general ability, learned skill, physical strength, time available, permission, necessity/obligation, intention, willingness, and apprehensive prediction. Read the attested **positive/negative pairs and ordering** in `aspect-modality.md`; `还`, `已`, `不`, or `要` can scope over another modal and change meaning. Verify whether the chosen form selects a verb, predicate phrase, or clause; an example of learned ability does not license prediction or emotional dependency. |
| Aspect and stem forms | Distinguish impending **onset** vs impending **completion**; prospective/irrealis stem changes; ongoing vs maintained state; 'still doing' vs 'not yet done'; perfective vs completed endpoint vs experiential vs current-result relevance; and change of state in property predicates. Check `aspect-modality.md` for source-backed examples (`xu`, `la`, `ɲie`, `tɕi`, `lie`, `liau`, etc.), then validate dialect, verb class, fusion, suffix slots, and polarity. A source adverb is not automatically an independent Tujia word or a universal suffix. |
| Linked events and direction | Identify the main event, the relation between events, and each participant's role: prior completion or preparation, manner/means/accompaniment, maintained background state, purpose, cause, or a direction/result construction. In Northern materials, compare perfective/sequential `liex`, manner `mor`, and background `bor` or `bor mor` with source examples; they are not interchangeable generic linkers. Distinguish perfective `liex` from directional `lier` even when both are written without tones as lie. Verify who moves and whether the construction can describe movement of the object rather than the subject. |
| Negation | Distinguish negative existence or possession, factual negation, intention or prediction negation, negative ability, prohibition, and negative copulas. Determine what is negated and whether a lexical negative predicate can combine with another negative or modal form. Preserve the scope of double negation; the availability of both components alone does not license their concatenation. |
| Nominalization and linking | Determine whether the construction refers to an event, proposition, participant, object, place, or a modifier of an explicit head. Check argument gaps, head position, and the distribution of linking and nominalizing forms. Different functions or homophonous markers are not freely interchangeable. |
| Property predicates and adjectives | Classify the actual lexical form and its use as predicate, attributive modifier, or independent nominal expression. A Chinese adjective gloss does not establish a Tujia adjective. Check short vs derived forms and whether modification requires a linker or nominalization. Neither the predicate form nor a related adjective can automatically substitute for the other. |

For Northern Tujia, start with the reference grammar's predicate overview, sections 4.2–4.7, predicate-component order, and chapter 6; use the corresponding Southern sections for Southern translation. For Northern event linkage, consult reference-grammar section 6.4 and Brassett et al. (2006), section 4.6.3 (pp. 66–67), sections 9.3.1–9.3.3 (pp. 151–154), and section 9.6 (pp. 157–159). `mor` can scope over a phrase, not just a verb; `liex` retains perfective meaning in sequences and is not a dedicated connective. A prior action presented as accomplished or preparatory may leave a state that continues during the next event, so timing alone does not decide the choice. Check aspect, focus, event structure, and actual examples together. Keep source-specific differences and conflicting analyses explicit. Productive rules can support a new sentence without an exact full-sentence match when their semantic and grammatical conditions fit. If applicability remains unresolved after the bounded local checks, choose the best-supported provisional construction, present it as a proposed translation, and locate the uncertainty explicitly rather than hiding it behind a fluent lexical paraphrase or Chinese insertion. Do not withhold the clause.

## Translate

1. **Analyze meaning by clause.** Track participants and their roles, reference, time, aspect, negation scope, modality, comparison, condition, direction, result, and speech act, including how successive or concurrent events relate. Keep narration and quotation distinct. Split long clauses where useful without deleting events or logical relations.
2. **Retrieve senses and classify lexical forms.** For Northern/Longshan, start with Brassett (2006), then check Brassett (2004), before relying on other surveys or corpus glosses; apply the mandatory source-priority rules above. Within that priority, prefer witnesses demonstrably from the target locality and compatible sources, and investigate any better-supported local counterevidence. Identify the source locality and limits of any cross-locality borrowing. Check context, argument roles, idioms, loans, and collocations. Search aligned passages for contextual senses missing from short dictionary labels, including ordinary people, tools, actions, and customary objects. Establish the candidate predicate's class from its grammar and usage, rather than relying on a Chinese gloss or a machine part-of-speech label. Search synonyms and simpler expressions without assuming they share the same grammatical behavior. For names and cultural terms, verify what the expression denotes in context; shared components or a literal gloss do not establish that a word for a motif also names the finished object.
3. **Validate grammar before composing.** Apply the priority checks above to each required modal, aspectual, negative, nominalizing, modifying, and event-linking operation. Find an applicable rule or close attested examples for the whole construction, including relevant combination restrictions. Verify phrase order, case or relation markers, modifier vs predicate forms, and clause linkage. General SOV order is a starting point; use the selected dialect's actual patterns for questions, topics, comparison, and other constructions.
4. **Apply supported rules.** Ordinary lexical substitution is possible when the construction is productive and the new word fits its semantic role. Check animacy, countability, classifiers, possession, compound boundaries, register, and tonal or morphological effects. When adapting an example, check additions, deletions, and movement separately; a gloss alone does not establish that a marker is optional in that construction. A superficially similar example does not establish a new predicate construction or suffix function.

   Choose classifiers by the Tujia head noun and its attested semantic class, not by a one-to-one match to a Chinese classifier. Search classifier senses, including original English descriptions, and count phrases in sentences. A Chinese gloss such as “只” or “株” can conceal a wider or narrower Tujia distribution. First use a suitable attested native classifier. When a new Chinese classifier is needed, check the borrowing pattern for the whole numeral–classifier phrase: an appropriate Chinese insertion can be “一株”, rather than an unverified mixture of native “one” and Chinese “株”. Preserve a supplied mixed-language form and identify its provenance; do not universalize one example into a rule for every borrowed classifier.
5. **Build the predicate.** Resolve action stage, direction, result, ability, intention, possibility, and negation before choosing forms. Distinguish the meanings behind source-language expressions such as Chinese “会” and “了”. Check the whole predicate, contractions, irregular forms, and the scope of each marker; use Southern evidence for Southern morphology.
6. **Realize the sentence.** Arrange arguments and modifiers, select attested full forms, and maintain consistent reference and discourse flow. Preserve meaningful repetition, address terms, and voice. For extended prose, consult same-register passages for topic continuity, pronoun use, parallel clauses, degree expressions, and repetition. Use attested productive patterns where applicable; a list of native words in a Chinese sentence frame does not establish Tujia narrative usage. For constrained genres, check rhythm and line structure after semantic and grammatical checks.
7. **Audit grammar and meaning.** First revisit the applicability and interaction of modality, aspect, negation, nominalization, predicate/modifier forms, and linked events with their movement participants in the assembled clause. Then recheck roles, polarity, time, comparison direction, quantity, reference, and omitted or added content against the source. Check lexical sense, dialect, tones, and spelling. Back-translation can expose drift; it does not establish grammaticality or naturalness.

## Paraphrase and borrowing

Use meaning-preserving paraphrase actively. Translate the intended sense rather than requiring a dictionary headword for every source expression. Search the semantic field across Chinese and English glosses and inspect actual arguments and collocations: a brief headword gloss may cover the intended sense under different wording. Incorporate lexical information supplied by the user with its stated provenance, and investigate its construction rather than repeating an exact-gloss search. Simplify ornate wording, split reciprocal statements into two clauses, and express an abstract relation through a supported construction. Preserve participants, polarity, scope, conditions, and the passage's central contrast. Figurative large numbers can become “many” when abundance is the point; “all” is suitable when the intended set is exhaustive. Keep exact quantities when they matter. Briefly identify a material shift in meaning or emphasis.

Before borrowing a whole compound or idiom, try expressing its meaning with simpler predicates, modality, negation, or two linked clauses. For abstract, literary, or technical concepts, also search common everyday synonyms, broader or narrower senses appropriate to the context, and familiar paired or opposing terms. A conceptual pair may be expressible with an ordinary word pair even when its abstract Chinese label has no exact dictionary match. Check each component's lexical form and the pair's use in the intended syntactic position; neither automatically add nominalization nor assume bare property predicates can always function as nouns. Check the scope of negation and modality in the resulting construction; independently attested component words do not make every combination interchangeable. Prefer a supported compositional native expression when it retains the intended relation and emphasis.

When a native expression is unavailable or unsuitable, consult same-dialect examples of Chinese loans and code-switching. Inspect what was borrowed—a noun, a predicate, a modifier, or a connector—and how it participates in Tujia syntax. Borrowed nouns still take the relevant noun-phrase order and classifiers; objects precede verbs where the selected construction requires SOV. Borrowed predicates must receive the selected dialect's appropriate argument order, negation, aspect, and modality. For a compositional Chinese verb–object expression, separate the object from the predicate before borrowing and rebuild the expression in Tujia order. When the construction is SOV, use subject + object + predicate, even if both lexical units remain in Chinese characters. Prefer a suitable attested Tujia verb when available; otherwise propose the missing noun and verb as separate insertions. Apply negation, modality, aspect, and any stem changes to the resulting predicate in their licensed positions, not to an opaque Chinese verb–object block. Preserve genuinely lexicalized compounds and names as lexical units only where their status and usage justify it.

Chinese lexical insertions may be written directly in Chinese characters. For a new translation, a proposed insertion can fill an unattested lexical slot when analogous borrowing patterns support the construction. Distinguish this proposed use from an established loan or a quoted mixed-language utterance; an exact historical occurrence of every inserted Chinese word is not a prerequisite for offering a clearly labelled mixed-language draft. Existing loans and proposed insertions remain subject to semantic and grammatical checks. Do not invent a Tujia pronunciation or tone for Chinese characters, or treat Chinese word order as the default merely because some words are Chinese.

Keep Tujia sentence structure and supported vocabulary wherever possible. Before delivery, revisit proposed Chinese insertions for native words missed through narrow gloss matching, and use a suitable native expression when found unless the task calls for borrowing. Explain the principal paraphrases and Chinese insertions once after the translation. A lexical gap that can be addressed this way need not become a placeholder; unresolved syntax or a materially uncertain meaning still requires a localized note **alongside a completed provisional rendering**.

For a long mixed-language draft, audit each proposed Chinese content-word insertion internally: the intended sense, relevant dictionary and continuous-text evidence, any suitable native candidate, and the remaining reason for insertion. Distinguish an extraction failure or incomplete search from an expression whose native realization remains unresolved. Recheck multiword insertions as phrases, including internal roles and modifier attachment. Use this audit to replace avoidable insertions and locate actual gaps; it does not impose a borrowing quota or require an exact corpus match for every new sentence. A blanket label such as “proposed translation” or “unreviewed” does not resolve an unsupported construction.

## Preserve transcription and uncertainty

- **Default to 调号拼音 + IPA for every translation**, with 调号拼音 before IPA and clause-aligned forms. **Only show 叶氏拼音 when explicitly requested by the user.** A direct user request for a different notation supersedes this default. Use the site's tone-marked romanization, not the source's Ye spelling, in the first line. Keep raw source transcription in internal evidence; quote it separately only if requested or indispensable to explaining a disputed form. Prefer attested Brassett-based forms for Northern/Longshan unless the requested locality or better direct evidence calls for another source. Use verified source-specific conversions and inspect failures instead of completing missing mappings by intuition.
- Preserve tone values, syllable boundaries, nasalization, and genuinely untoned particles in source quotations. Keep source IPA unchanged when a display system groups distinct tone values. Different notation arrays are paired only when their provenance supports that pairing.
- Distinguish an exact quotation from a new translation. A new sentence whose words and productive construction have evidence may be offered as a **proposed translation / 拟译** even if the full sentence is unattested. State the specific remaining gap rather than requiring an exact corpus match for every sentence.
- After considering paraphrase and an appropriate Chinese insertion, give the **entire clause** a best-supported proposed rendering and mark remaining unsupported operations `⟦待核：…⟧`. If uncertainty affects the whole clause's roles, polarity, modality, or logic, still provide one clearly delimited provisional candidate **plus** a structural note; never replace the requested translation with an explanation or assert that the candidate is verified.
- Treat conflicting glosses, ambiguous alignment, missing tones, and incomplete source context as separate evidence gaps. Preserve ambiguity when it cannot be resolved. Identify Chinese insertions and retain their written form without assigning an unsupported Tujia pronunciation.

## Deliver

**Give the complete translation first, with every source sentence or line represented in order. This is mandatory even when retrieval, verification, or grammar checks are incomplete.** **Default deliverable: each translated clause must appear twice, once as 调号拼音 and once as matching IPA; omit 叶氏拼音 unless explicitly requested.** For a short text, add only notes needed to explain a material uncertainty or adaptation. For a longer text, align numbered clauses or passages with the source; keep dialect versions distinct. Use separate labeled lines (调号拼音 / IPA) or a two-column aligned table. Keep uncertainty markers localized in both representations when necessary and explain them afterward; never end at a research report. Add detailed word alignment or evidence tables only when the task calls for learning, review, or verification.

For an audit, identify the affected span, the specific issue, supporting evidence, and a correction or unresolved choice. Report what was actually checked. Reserve “native-speaker reviewed” for an actual documented review; corpus support alone does not establish naturalness.

Use committed or authorized material within its access scope. Permission to read source material for research does not authorize publishing it. Keep staged progress updates separate from the final translation text, so they do not interrupt its sentence alignment.
