# Clause analysis and grammatical validation

Read for a complex clause, revision, or detailed grammatical audit. Start with the short [translation workflow](../SKILL.md); use the [grammar index](grammar-index.md) to select examples. [Source priority](evidence.md) and [output format](output.md) have separate references.

## Establish the task

- Follow the requested dialect and locality. Reuse an unambiguous target from the conversation; if unspecified, default provisionally to Northern/Longshan and state that assumption once **without pausing for clarification**. For Northern without a narrower target, use Longshan materials and state that basis once. Translate Northern and Southern separately when both are requested.
- Identify the audience, register, and any **explicit** notation override; otherwise set the output to **调号拼音 + IPA**, never Ye romanization. Separate meaning from additional constraints such as meter, rhyme, line count, or terminology; explain a trade-off only when it affects the result.
- For revision, preserve the supplied text and edit the requested span. Distinguish the author's choices from independently attested forms.

## Validate grammatical applicability

Before composing a clause or adopting a paraphrase, verify the grammatical operations it needs in the target variety. **Classify source-language adverbs and auxiliaries semantically before looking them up as separate vocabulary:** e.g. '快要做' (imminent onset) vs '快做完' (imminent endpoint); '还在' vs '还没' vs '还不必'; '会' (trained skill, possibility, prediction) vs '可以' (permission); '已经' (current result) vs '做过' (experience). Follow [grammar-index.md](grammar-index.md) to the relevant aspect/modality, event, or clause subsection first; use [notation-audit.md](notation-audit.md) if spellings disagree. Prioritize modality, aspect and stem changes, negation, nominalization, predicate class, and relations between linked events. For each operation, identify its intended meaning and scope, the class of predicate or phrase it selects, its attachment site and order, and any restrictions on combinations. Consult the relevant grammar discussion and its examples; a dictionary gloss or a list of suffixes establishes neither applicability nor co-occurrence.

| Area | Check before use |
| --- | --- |
| Modality | Distinguish general ability, learned skill, physical strength, time available, permission, necessity/obligation, intention, willingness, and apprehensive prediction. Read the attested **positive/negative pairs and ordering** in [aspect-modality.md](aspect-modality.md); `还`, `已`, `不`, or `要` can scope over another modal and change meaning. Verify whether the chosen form selects a verb, predicate phrase, or clause; an example of learned ability does not license prediction or emotional dependency. |
| Aspect and stem forms | Distinguish impending **onset** vs impending **completion**; prospective/irrealis stem changes; ongoing vs maintained state; 'still doing' vs 'not yet done'; perfective vs completed endpoint vs experiential vs current-result relevance; and change of state in property predicates. Check [aspect-modality.md](aspect-modality.md) for source-backed examples (`xu`, `la`, `ɲie`, `tɕi`, `lie`, `liau`, etc.), then validate dialect, verb class, fusion, suffix slots, and polarity. A source adverb is not automatically an independent Tujia word or a universal suffix. |
| Linked events and direction | Identify the main event, the relation between events, and each participant's role: prior completion or preparation, manner/means/accompaniment, maintained background state, purpose, cause, or a direction/result construction. In Northern materials, compare perfective/sequential `liex`, manner `mor`, and background `bor` or `bor mor` with source examples; they are not interchangeable generic linkers. Distinguish perfective `liex` from directional `lier` even when both are written without tones as lie. Verify who moves and whether the construction can describe movement of the object rather than the subject. |
| Negation | Distinguish negative existence or possession, factual negation, intention or prediction negation, negative ability, prohibition, and negative copulas. Determine what is negated and whether a lexical negative predicate can combine with another negative or modal form. Preserve the scope of double negation; the availability of both components alone does not license their concatenation. |
| Nominalization and linking | Determine whether the construction refers to an event, proposition, participant, object, place, or a modifier of an explicit head. Check argument gaps, head position, and the distribution of linking and nominalizing forms. Different functions or homophonous markers are not freely interchangeable. |
| Property predicates and adjectives | Classify the actual lexical form and its use as predicate, attributive modifier, or independent nominal expression. A Chinese adjective gloss does not establish a Tujia adjective. Check short vs derived forms and whether modification requires a linker or nominalization. Neither the predicate form nor a related adjective can automatically substitute for the other. |

For Northern Tujia, start with the reference grammar's predicate overview, sections 4.2–4.7, predicate-component order, and chapter 6; use the corresponding Southern sections for Southern translation. For Northern event linkage, consult reference-grammar section 6.4 and Brassett et al. (2006), section 4.6.3 (pp. 66–67), sections 9.3.1–9.3.3 (pp. 151–154), and section 9.6 (pp. 157–159). `mor` can scope over a phrase, not just a verb; `liex` retains perfective meaning in sequences and is not a dedicated connective. A prior action presented as accomplished or preparatory may leave a state that continues during the next event, so timing alone does not decide the choice. Check aspect, focus, event structure, and actual examples together. Keep source-specific differences and conflicting analyses explicit. Productive rules can support a new sentence without an exact full-sentence match when their semantic and grammatical conditions fit. If applicability remains unresolved after the bounded local checks, complete the clause using the best-supported provisional construction and explain any essential uncertainty in a concise note.

## Compose and audit

1. **Analyze meaning by clause.** Track participants and their roles, reference, time, aspect, negation scope, modality, comparison, condition, direction, result, and speech act, including how successive or concurrent events relate. Keep narration and quotation distinct. Split long clauses where useful while carrying the intended relationships. For poetry and songs, identify the central image, voice and emotional movement, then use an expressive paraphrase suited to the requested style.
2. **Retrieve senses and classify lexical forms.** For Northern/Longshan, start with Brassett (2006), then check Brassett (2004), before relying on other surveys or corpus glosses; apply the [source-priority rules](evidence.md). Within that priority, prefer witnesses demonstrably from the target locality and compatible sources, and investigate any better-supported local counterevidence. Identify the source locality and limits of any cross-locality borrowing. Check context, argument roles, idioms, loans, and collocations. Search aligned passages for contextual senses missing from short dictionary labels, including ordinary people, tools, actions, and customary objects. Establish the candidate predicate's class from its grammar and usage, rather than relying on a Chinese gloss or a machine part-of-speech label. Search synonyms and simpler expressions without assuming they share the same grammatical behavior. For names and cultural terms, verify what the expression denotes in context; shared components or a literal gloss do not establish that a word for a motif also names the finished object.
3. **Validate grammar before composing.** Apply the priority checks above to each required modal, aspectual, negative, nominalizing, modifying, and event-linking operation. Find an applicable rule or close attested examples for the whole construction, including relevant combination restrictions. Verify phrase order, case or relation markers, modifier vs predicate forms, and clause linkage. General SOV order is a starting point; use the selected dialect's actual patterns for questions, topics, comparison, and other constructions.
4. **Apply supported rules.** Ordinary lexical substitution is possible when the construction is productive and the new word fits its semantic role. Check animacy, countability, classifiers, possession, compound boundaries, register, and tonal or morphological effects. When adapting an example, check additions, deletions, and movement separately; a gloss alone does not establish that a marker is optional in that construction. A superficially similar example does not establish a new predicate construction or suffix function.

   Choose classifiers by the Tujia head noun and its attested semantic class, not by a one-to-one match to a Chinese classifier. Search classifier senses, including original English descriptions, and count phrases in sentences. A Chinese gloss such as “只” or “株” can conceal a wider or narrower Tujia distribution. First use a suitable attested native classifier. When a new Chinese classifier is needed, check the borrowing pattern for the whole numeral–classifier phrase: an appropriate Chinese insertion can be “一株”, rather than an unverified mixture of native “one” and Chinese “株”. Preserve a supplied mixed-language form and identify its provenance; do not universalize one example into a rule for every borrowed classifier.
5. **Build the predicate.** Resolve action stage, direction, result, ability, intention, possibility, and negation before choosing forms. Distinguish the meanings behind source-language expressions such as Chinese “会” and “了”. Check the whole predicate, contractions, irregular forms, and the scope of each marker; use Southern evidence for Southern morphology.
6. **Realize the sentence.** Arrange arguments and modifiers, select attested full forms, and maintain consistent reference and discourse flow. Preserve meaningful repetition, address terms, and voice. For extended prose, consult same-register passages for topic continuity, pronoun use, parallel clauses, degree expressions, and repetition. Use attested productive patterns where applicable; a list of native words in a Chinese sentence frame does not establish Tujia narrative usage. For constrained genres, check rhythm and line structure after semantic and grammatical checks.
7. **Audit grammar and meaning.** First revisit the applicability and interaction of modality, aspect, negation, nominalization, predicate/modifier forms, and linked events with their movement participants in the assembled clause. Then recheck roles, polarity, time, comparison direction, quantity and reference against the intended reading. Assess poetic transformations at the level of imagery and emotional relation. Reconcile unused suitable lexical candidates with the draft using [evidence.md](evidence.md#retrieval-status-and-draft-reconciliation). Check lexical sense, dialect, tones and spelling. Use back-translation to inspect meaning, and source constructions to assess grammar.
