# Clause analysis and grammatical validation

Read for a complex clause, revision, or detailed grammatical audit. Start with the short [translation workflow](../SKILL.md); use the [grammar index](grammar-index.md) to select examples. [Source priority](evidence.md) and [output format](output.md) have separate references.

## Establish the task

- Use the requested dialect and locality, including an unambiguous target established in the conversation. With no narrower target, proceed provisionally with Northern/Longshan materials and state that basis once. Translate Northern and Southern separately when both are requested.
- Identify audience, register and any notation override, then apply [output](output.md). Separate meaning from meter, rhyme, line count and terminology; explain a trade-off when it affects the result.
- For revision, preserve the supplied text and edit the requested span. Distinguish the author's choices from independently attested forms.

## Validate grammatical applicability

Before composing or adopting a paraphrase, classify source-language adverbs and auxiliaries by meaning: '快要做' (imminent onset) vs '快做完' (imminent endpoint); '还在' vs '还没' vs '还不必'; '会' (trained skill, possibility, prediction) vs '可以' (permission); '已经' (current result) vs '做过' (experience). Use the [grammar index](grammar-index.md) to find the relevant discussion and examples. For each operation, establish meaning, scope, selected predicate or phrase, attachment, order and combination restrictions. The checks below establish grammatical applicability beyond a dictionary gloss or suffix list.

| Area | Check before use |
| --- | --- |
| Modality | Distinguish general ability, learned skill, physical strength, time available, permission, necessity/obligation, intention, willingness, and apprehensive prediction. Read the attested **positive/negative pairs and ordering** in [aspect-modality.md](aspect-modality.md); `还`, `已`, `不`, or `要` can scope over another modal and change meaning. Select a form whose attested modal sense and complement type—verb, predicate phrase or clause—match the intended reading. |
| Aspect and stem forms | Distinguish impending **onset** vs impending **completion**; prospective/irrealis stem changes; ongoing vs maintained state; 'still doing' vs 'not yet done'; perfective vs completed endpoint vs experiential vs current-result relevance; and change of state in property predicates. Check [aspect-modality.md](aspect-modality.md) for source-backed examples (`xu`, `la`, `ɲie`, `tɕi`, `lie`, `liau`, etc.). Select the attested realization—independent word, stem form or suffix—and validate dialect, verb class, fusion, suffix slots and polarity. |
| Linked events and direction | Use [event relations](event-linking.md#event-relations) to select clause linkage, and [directional constructions](direction-suffixes.md) for movement and spatial relations. |
| Negation | Distinguish negative existence or possession, factual negation, intention or prediction negation, negative ability, prohibition, and negative copulas. Determine what is negated and check whole-predicate evidence for combinations of lexical negative predicates with other negative or modal forms. Match the intended double-negation scope. |
| Nominalization and linking | Determine whether the construction refers to an event, proposition, participant, object, place, or a modifier of an explicit head. Select each linking or nominalizing form by its attested function, argument gap, head position and referent. |
| Property predicates and adjectives | Classify the lexical form from its grammar and usage as a predicate, attributive modifier or independent nominal expression. Select the attested short or derived form for that role, checking any required linker or nominalization. |

Use the cached [predicate constructions](aspect-modality.md), [relation-marker frames](clause-patterns.md), [nominalization choices](nie-niexi.md) and [event-linking patterns](event-linking.md). Match roles, scope and predicate class, then apply the supported construction directly. Resolve a new source conflict through [notation audit](notation-audit.md); preserve established rules in their topics. For broader context, use the Northern predicate overview, reference-grammar §§4.2–4.7 and chapter 6, or the corresponding Southern sections. Use [recovery](recovery-playbook.md) for unresolved applicability.

## Scope and semantic roles

For nested quotation, complements and waiting constructions, check each clause level separately: identify its speaker or subject, predicate, embedded event, and the event or participant selected by each negative or modal operator. Track factual negation, permission, ability and necessity through the matrix and embedded clauses. Preserve these attachments when reordering or nominalizing a clause, including whether an operator applies to waiting or to the awaited event.

For questions, use the [interrogative constructions](clause-patterns.md) to preserve the intended answer target.

## Compose and audit

Analyze meaning and retrieve senses through the [shared semantic plan](semantic-decomposition.md), then use [predicate economy](predicate-economy.md) to compose. Validate the assembled clause against the selected productive rule or complete example, checking argument order, predicate class, combination restrictions and any changed, added or omitted elements. Review the result through [coverage](sense-and-coverage.md), [reference alignment](reference-first-check.md) and [notation audit](notation-audit.md).

Choose classifiers by the Tujia head noun's attested semantic class. Search original English descriptions and count phrases: glosses such as “只” or “株” may conceal a wider or narrower distribution. Prefer a suitable native classifier. For a new Chinese classifier, check borrowing of the whole numeral–classifier phrase; “一株” may be supported where native “one” + Chinese “株” is unverified. Preserve a supplied mixed-language form with its provenance and the attested scope of its borrowing pattern.

Preserve meaningful repetition, address terms and voice. In extended prose, consult same-register passages for topic continuity, pronouns, parallel clauses, degree expressions and repetition.

Match remaining Chinese content words to the [retrieved candidate record](evidence.md#retrieval-status-and-draft-reconciliation). For poetry and songs, verify the intended image, voice and emotional relation using [paraphrase choices](paraphrase-and-borrowing.md#express-the-intended-meaning), then check rhythm and line structure.

Back-translation can expose semantic drift; grammaticality and naturalness require their own evidence.
