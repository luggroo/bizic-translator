# Paraphrase and Chinese insertions

Read when composing a paraphrase or filling a lexical gap. Follow the [complete-version format](output.md#deliver-the-requested-result), the [shared semantic plan](semantic-decomposition.md), [source evidence](evidence.md) and the [grammar index](grammar-index.md).

## Express the intended meaning

Prefer an attested equivalent native word or productive native construction; use established same-dialect loans with their recorded forms and usage. Preserve event type, participant roles, polarity, scope, clause relations and central contrast.

<a id="noun-descriptions"></a>
When replacing a noun with a descriptive phrase, check its category, semantic range and added properties. Preserve the intended class and referent, with the same breadth and only the restrictions supported by the source.

Search Chinese and English glosses, everyday synonyms, appropriate broader or narrower senses, and familiar paired or opposing terms. Inspect each candidate's arguments and collocations; a short headword gloss may cover the intended sense under different wording. Incorporate user-supplied lexical information with its stated provenance and investigate the corresponding construction.

Use simpler predicates, modality, negation or linked clauses to express a compound or abstract relation. Choose nominalization, an explicit head or a property predicate according to the intended syntactic role. Check the scope of negation and modality in the assembled expression. Figurative large numbers can become “many” when abundance is the point; use “all” for an exhaustive set and retain exact quantities when they matter.

For poetry and songs, use interpretive paraphrase, an explicit metaphor or a recast image when it carries the passage's emotional force, speaker–addressee relationship and central image. Judge these choices at the level of the line and passage. A metaphor expressed through a transformation, posture or state can be a valid literary rendering. In an explicit audit, describe the expressive effect and assess lexical and grammatical support separately. Explain a change in meaning when it materially affects the reader's understanding.

## Fill remaining lexical slots

After the bounded source checks, place Chinese words into the best-supported Tujia construction. Consult same-dialect examples of loans and code-switching for nouns, predicates, modifiers and connectors. Match noun-phrase order and classifiers to the selected construction. Arrange objects and predicates in the dialect's applicable SOV or marked frame, and attach negation, aspect, modality and stem changes in their licensed positions.

For a compositional Chinese verb–object expression, identify the object and predicate separately and rebuild their relation in Tujia order. Prefer a suitable attested Tujia verb. A missing noun or verb can remain in Chinese characters. Keep established lexicalized compounds and names together when their usage supports that treatment. Use a whole Chinese numeral–classifier phrase when that is the supported borrowing pattern.

Write proposed Chinese insertions as plain Chinese characters, using their script as the visual distinction. Supply Tujia pronunciation and tone for source-supported forms. Keep the provenance of established loans, quoted usage and newly proposed insertions in the internal evidence record. Productive analogous patterns can support a new insertion; a complete proposed clause can also use the best-supported provisional arrangement with a concise note about any essential construction uncertainty.

## Reconcile candidates with the completed draft

For each remaining Chinese content word or phrase, compare its intended sense with the retrieved dictionary and continuous-text candidates. Use a suitable Tujia expression when available, following the task's stylistic choices. Record the candidate's attestation, its suitability and the reason for the final selection. Inspect internal roles and modifier attachment in multiword insertions as well as the surrounding clause.

Use the [retrieval statuses](evidence.md#retrieval-status-and-draft-reconciliation) to distinguish search coverage, candidate suitability and unresolved lexical expression. Review syntax, marker function and literary paraphrase on their own dimensions. Present the complete translation using the [output format](output.md); retain the detailed reconciliation for a requested audit.
