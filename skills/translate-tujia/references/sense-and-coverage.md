# Sense, context and translated coverage

Use this check while choosing words and immediately before delivery. Keep the working notes internal unless the reader requests an audit. Apply the same steps to embedding and static retrieval.

## Choose a sense together with its frame

For each content word, identify the intended referent, event or relation and its role in the clause. Match the complete attestation's Chinese and English senses, part of speech, usage conditions and example to that use. For predicates, include participants, intentionality, event type and result. Select the witness whose full sense and frame fit; resolve remaining ambiguity through the bounded query plan.

When an exact query misses, preserve the intended sense and try a common lexical variant, a longer everyday headword, a simple paraphrase or an available English equivalent. Apply the usual bounded query budget. Record which expansion returned the useful witness so a missing spelling variant remains distinguishable from a missing concept.

## Put retrieved evidence into the sentence

Maintain a short coverage ledger: meaning unit → chosen witness → actual target span. Include predicates and their arguments, pronouns, numbers, classifiers, comparison standards, locations and relations. For a rejected candidate, retain the specific sense or source mismatch.

Build an unfamiliar phrase from supported parts. Keep its attested words and grammatical frame in the draft, with the smallest remaining Chinese insertion or marked gap. Check lexical support and construction support separately: uncertainty in one suffix or attachment leaves the supported noun, number or predicate available for use. Mark an unverified construction at its precise span and preserve the intended semantic relation.

Before declaring a lexical gap, check the original query, the bounded expansions and already returned candidates. Use “not retrieved” for a failed search, “retrieved but not adopted” for an unused suitable witness, and “not established in the checked resources” when the available evidence remains insufficient. These diagnoses guide different next steps.

## Preserve the communicative and event context

Identify whether a short expression is an address, agreement, permission, evaluation, instruction or assertion. Select a witness with a compatible relationship, register and discourse function. Carry contextual speaker preferences forward; retain a source-specific usage warning as a local warning rather than a universal substitution rule.

For a chain of actions, keep a compact event map: participant and object → movement and destination → subsequent action → resulting state. Anchor direction to the speaker or established narrative viewpoint. Anchor aspect and modality to the relevant event, including planned steps, previous experience and current results. Read the resulting clauses back against this map and the original negation or focus scope.

Finish by checking each item in the coverage ledger against the actual wording. State any meaning lost through a paraphrase and any uncertainty that still affects a core predicate or relation. Deliver under the shared [output policy](output.md).
